A conversation, not a questionnaire.

Follow-ups probe depth and confidence. A shallow answer and a detailed one lead to different next questions.

Question

How do you decide whether a metric change in an experiment is real?

Answer

I would check the p-value and ship if it is under 0.05.

Follow-up from SPHERO

What if you looked at twenty metrics and only this one crossed the line? How would that change your call?

Evidence update: Experiment design: still needs evidence. Sphero probes multiple testing and practical significance.

Sample exchange. The follow-up depends on the answer, not a fixed list.

Four kinds of question.

  • Concept

    Can you explain it plainly and correctly?

  • Applied reasoning

    Can you use it on a realistic problem?

  • Trade-off

    Do you know what you give up with each choice?

  • Role-round simulation

    Only where your target process includes that round.

What you leave with.

  • A grounded capability map
  • Confirmed gaps
  • Uncertain areas
  • Your first next-best action

Rules of the room.

  • You can pause and return. Voice or video is optional.
  • Only role-relevant demonstrated capability is evaluated. No grade on your intelligence.
  • No emotion or face analysis, and no unsupported psychological inference.
  • You can inspect the evidence behind any result and challenge it.
  • Rubric dimensions: Correctness, Reasoning, Trade-offs, Structure, Communication.
  • What is captured and how long it is kept is stated before you begin.
SPHERO guidance and evaluations can be wrong. Treat them as evidence-informed input and review important decisions yourself. Nothing here is a hiring guarantee.
